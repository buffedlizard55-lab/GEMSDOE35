Understanding the 0.2778 Score & Exceeding 0.3262

1. Mathematical Mechanics of the Distance-Weighted Tversky Index (DTI)

The competition evaluates predictions via the Distance-Weighted Tversky Index: $$DTI = \frac{TP_{weighted}}{TP_{weighted} + \alpha FP_{weighted} + \beta FN_{weighted}} \quad (\alpha=0.2, \beta=0.8)$$ using a 300 m Euclidean triangular kernel $k(d) = \max(1 - d/300\text{ m}, 0)$ where 300 m corresponds to 3 pixels on the 100 m grid. Because $TP_{weighted}(g) = \max_{x \in P} p(x) k(d(x,g))$, placing a dense solid 1-pixel raster line provides full credit but accumulates massive false positive mass ($FP_{weighted} = \sum p(x)[1 - \max_g k(d(x,g))]$) whenever predictions miss unmapped targets by $>300$ m.

2. The Breakthrough: Spatial Sub-Nyquist Lattice ($d \approx 2.5 - 2.8$ px) and Core Flank-Pruning

Across the GEMSDOE series, scores jumped from 0.1892 (continuous ridges) to 0.2477 ($d=1.5$ px), 0.2600 ($d=2.8$ px), 0.2708 (solo dotted $d=2.8$ px), and 0.2778 (GEMSDOE32 H33-2-B2). At spacing $d \approx 2.8$ pixels (~280 m), any ground-truth fault segment remains within ≤140 m of a predicted point ($k(d) \ge 0.533$ to $1.0$), capturing ~85% of $TP$ credit while reducing total emitted positive mass by ~65%. H33-2-B2 achieved 0.2778 by truncating low-confidence flank pixels to zero, cutting diffuse background $FP$ penalty.

3. How to Surpass 0.2778 and Reach Top Leaderboard Scores (> 0.3262)

To exceed 0.2778 and beat the current leaderboard leader (0.3262):

  • Add Independent Geomorphic Scarp Curvature (H35-06): Quaternary fault scarps directly express surface tectonic rupture. H35-06 passed our spatial promotion gate with +0.0146 mean ΔDTI vs baseline and +0.0083 vs H35-01 across 5/6 spatial tiles.
  • Transtensional Strain Dilatation (H35-08): Over 90% of Great Basin geothermal systems occur in dilatational stepovers and fault tips where positive geodetic dilatation ($\dot{\varepsilon}_{dil} > 0$) maintains open fluid pathways.
  • Tilt Derivative AGC Edge Normalization (H35-07): Tilt angle horizontal derivatives normalize deep/shallow and weak/strong magnetic contacts, surfacing weak blind fault contacts drowned out by volcanic anomalies.
  • Optimal DTI Point Lattice Regularization: Tuning point emission geometry to the exact 300 m triangular support radius.
